🔍 Analysis: Good News and Bad News

The Good News

  • Alcohol-related violent crime is down 35% since 2000 (from 28,000 to 18,000 suspects)
  • Per-capita alcohol consumption dropped from 12.5L to 9.5L pure alcohol per year
  • Public drunkenness arrests have halved since 2005
  • Drunk driving deaths fell from 120 (2000) to 45 (2023)

The Bad News

  • Drug-related crime is up 180% since 2000
  • "Mixed intoxication" (alcohol + drugs) crimes doubled since 2015
  • Young suspects (15-24) increasingly involve non-alcohol substances
  • Property crime clearance rates are falling as suspects are harder to identify

The Substitution Effect

As alcohol consumption declines, other substances are filling the gap:

  • Cannabis: Found in 45% of drug-related crime suspects
  • Amphetamines: 32% (highly associated with property crime)
  • Benzodiazepines: 18% (often combined with alcohol)
  • Cocaine: Rising rapidly (3x increase since 2015)

The Gender Gap

For alcohol-related violent crime:

  • Men: 89% of suspects (stable over 25 years)
  • Women: 11% of suspects (slowly increasing)

🤔 The Policy Debate

Prohibitionists argue: "62% of violent crimes involve alcohol - we need stricter alcohol policy"

Libertarians counter: "Alcohol-related crime dropped 35% while consumption dropped 25% - current policy is working"

The data suggests: Finland's high alcohol taxes and restricted sales hours correlate with declining alcohol harm, but new challenges from other substances require different approaches.
